FAQ

How much does mobile app design cost, and is it cheaper if I already have a web app?

Costs depend on the app’s complexity and required features. If you have a web app with established design elements, we may be able to reuse components, reducing design effort and cost. We’ll review your existing app and provide a tailored estimate based on what can be adapted.

Can you start with just a few screens and then redesign the whole app later?

Yes, many clients begin with a few key screens to validate features and gather feedback before expanding. This phased approach allows you to refine the design while managing costs. Once the initial screens are approved, we can proceed with a full redesign as needed.

What is your typical timeline for designing a mobile app?

Timelines vary based on complexity, screen count, and feedback cycles, but most projects take 4–8 weeks. More complex apps may require additional time. We’ll establish a clear timeline at the start and keep you informed throughout the process.

How will the functionality differ between web and mobile?

Mobile apps can leverage GPS, camera, and push notifications, offering unique capabilities beyond a web app. The design also adapts for mobile-specific interactions like swiping and tapping. We ensure the best use of mobile features while maintaining consistency with your web version.

Will the user experience be significantly different between web and mobile?

While the user experience will be optimized for mobile devices, we ensure consistency between the web and mobile versions of your app. The core design elements, branding, and user flows are maintained across both platforms so that users will feel a smooth experience regardless of the device.

Can you estimate the project based on our existing wireframes?

Yes, we can provide an initial estimate based on your wireframes, user flows, and documentation. These assets help define the scope and effort required. Once we review the details, we’ll refine the estimate and timeline accordingly.

Can you skip wireframes and UX and update only the UI?

We can, but we recommend at least reviewing the UX to ensure usability. Wireframes serve as a blueprint, helping to refine user flows before committing to visuals. Skipping this step could lead to usability issues and costly revisions later.
